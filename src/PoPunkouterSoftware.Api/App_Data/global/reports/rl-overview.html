Coaching Styles — "How Are They Taught?"

Why a stroller graduates in 16 minutes while boxing or 11v11 football takes overnight runs: it comes down to the coaching format.

Solo PracticeFastest to Learn
PoOlympic PoRobotTrain

One agent practices against the clock, a target pose, or a ramp—no unpredictable opponent trying to trick it.

Why it matters: Great for building clean, reusable building-block skills (Sprint, Crawl, Get-Up, Head Tracking).

1-on-1 Sparring (Self-Play)Arms Race
PoBox PoSlapUnreal PoSumo

Two fighters learn by battling older versions of themselves. Every time one finds a cheap trick, the other learns to counter it.

Why it matters: Takes longer (overnight runs) because the opponent keeps getting smarter, but produces exciting reactive combat.

Pack / Free-for-AllChaos & Crowds
PoHillKing PoRaceUnreal

Multiple bodies shove, pass, or block each other in the same space. Agents must handle bumps and traffic.

Why it matters: Teaches sturdy balance and collision recovery—ideal for party games and racing.

Team Sport CoachingHardest to Master
PoBasketball PoFootball PoSoccer

Teammates share goals (scoring, defending, passing) while coordinating around a moving ball.

Why it matters: Requires multi-agent credit assignment (MA-POCA or role directors) so players pass instead of chasing the ball in a clump.
